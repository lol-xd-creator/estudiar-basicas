# 9 · Estilo indirecto y verbos de reporte

El **estilo indirecto** (*reported speech*) sirve para contar lo que otro dijo sin citarlo textualmente: *"I'm ready"* → *She said she **was** ready*. El examen pregunta tres cosas: el **corrimiento de tiempos**, el orden de las **preguntas indirectas** (sin inversión ni *do*) y los **patrones de los verbos de reporte** (*suggest*, *advise*, *deny*...).

::: claves
- **Corrimiento de tiempos** (*backshift*): si el verbo que reporta está en pasado (*said*), los tiempos dan un paso atrás: *am → was*, *will → would*, *have done → had done*, *did → had done*.
- **También cambian** pronombres, tiempo y lugar: *today → that day*, *tomorrow → the next day*, *here → there*.
- ***say*** contra ***tell***: *tell* **siempre** lleva a quién: *He **told me***. *He said me* ✗.
- **Preguntas indirectas:** orden de afirmación, sin *do* ni signo de pregunta: *She asked **where the logs were***. Sí/no → ***if*** o ***whether***.
- **Órdenes y pedidos:** *tell/ask* + persona + ***(not) to*** + base.
- **Patrones de los verbos de reporte:** *suggest **doing*** (nunca *suggest someone to*), *advise someone **to***, *deny **doing***, *apologise **for doing***, *insist **on doing***.
:::

## Directo e indirecto

| Estilo directo | Estilo indirecto |
|---|---|
| *"I **am** working on the bug," she said.* | *She said (that) she **was** working on the bug.* |
| *"We **will** finish today," he said.* | *He said (that) they **would** finish that day.* |

***that*** después de *say* o *tell* se puede omitir.

## El corrimiento de tiempos

Cuando el verbo que reporta está en **pasado** (*said, told, explained*), lo que se reporta "retrocede" un tiempo, porque ya no es el momento en que se dijo.

| Lo que dijo (directo) | Lo que se reporta (indirecto) |
|---|---|
| *Present simple:* "I **work** remotely." | *Past simple:* She said she **worked** remotely. |
| *Present continuous:* "**I'm testing** it." | *Past continuous:* He said he **was testing** it. |
| *Present perfect:* "**I've fixed** it." | *Past perfect:* She said she **had fixed** it. |
| *Past simple:* "I **sent** the email." | *Past perfect:* He said he **had sent** the email. (o *sent*) |
| ***will***: "**I'll call** you." | ***would***: She said she **would call** me. |
| ***can***: "I **can** help." | ***could***: He said he **could** help. |
| ***may***: "It **may** fail." | ***might***: She said it **might** fail. |
| ***must***: "You **must** sign it." | ***had to*** (o *must*): He said I **had to** sign it. |
| ***is going to***: "**It's going to** crash." | ***was going to***: She said it **was going to** crash. |

**No cambian:** *would, could, should, might, ought to* y el *past perfect*.

### Cuándo no hace falta correr los tiempos

- Si el verbo que reporta está en **presente**: *She **says** she **is** busy.*
- Si lo que se dijo **sigue siendo verdad**: *He said Python **is** easy to learn* (también vale *was*).
- Si se reporta enseguida: *I just talked to Ana. She said **she's** coming.*

En un examen, si el verbo que reporta está en pasado y no hay indicios de lo contrario, la respuesta segura es **correr el tiempo**.

## Pronombres, tiempo y lugar

Las palabras que dependen de **quién habla, cuándo y dónde** también cambian:

| Directo | Indirecto |
|---|---|
| *today* | *that day* |
| *tonight* | *that night* |
| *tomorrow* | *the next day / the following day* |
| *yesterday* | *the day before / the previous day* |
| *next week* | *the following week* |
| *last week* | *the week before / the previous week* |
| *two days ago* | *two days before / earlier* |
| *now* | *then / at that moment* |
| *here* | *there* |
| *this / these* | *that / those* |

*"**I'll** review **your** code **tomorrow**," she told me.* → *She told me **she would** review **my** code **the next day**.*

## *say* contra *tell*

| Verbo | Estructura | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| ***say*** | *say* (*that*)... / *say something **to** someone* | *She **said** (that) she was tired.* / *He **said** hello **to** me.* |
| ***tell*** | *tell **someone*** (*that*)... — **siempre** con la persona | *She **told me** (that) she was tired.* |

::: ojo
- *He **said me** that...* ✗ → *He **told me** that...* ✓ / *He **said to me** that...* ✓.
- *She **told that**...* ✗ → *She **said that**...* ✓.
- **Frases fijas con *tell*** (sin persona): ***tell the truth***, ***tell a lie***, ***tell a story***, ***tell the difference***, ***tell the time***.
:::

## Preguntas indirectas

Al reportar una pregunta, **deja de ser pregunta**: el orden vuelve a ser **sujeto + verbo**, desaparece ***do/does/did*** y no hay signo de pregunta. Los tiempos se corren igual.

| Tipo | Directo | Indirecto |
|---|---|---|
| Con palabra interrogativa | *"Where **is** the server?"* | *He asked where **the server was**.* |
| | *"What time **does** the meeting **start**?"* | *She asked what time **the meeting started**.* |
| | *"Why **did** you **change** the config?"* | *They asked me why **I had changed** the config.* |
| Sí o no | *"**Do** you **use** Docker?"* | *She asked **if / whether I used** Docker.* |
| | *"**Have** you **finished**?"* | *He asked **if I had finished**.* |

::: ojo
**Preguntas indirectas de cortesía.** Con *Could you tell me...?*, *Do you know...?*, *I wonder...* o *I'd like to know...*, la segunda parte **no** se invierte, aunque haya signo de pregunta al final:

| Mal | Bien |
|---|---|
| *Could you tell me where **is the meeting room**?* | *Could you tell me where **the meeting room is**?* |
| *Do you know what time **does it start**?* | *Do you know what time **it starts**?* |
| *I wonder why **did the build fail**.* | *I wonder why **the build failed**.* |

**Excepción:** si la palabra interrogativa es el **sujeto**, el orden ya es el normal: *Do you know **who broke** the build?*
:::

## Órdenes, pedidos y consejos

Se reportan con un verbo + **persona** + ***(not) to*** + base:

| Directo | Indirecto |
|---|---|
| *"Restart the server."* | *She **told me to restart** the server.* |
| *"Please, send me the report."* | *He **asked me to send** him the report.* |
| *"**Don't** touch production."* | *They **told us not to touch** production.* |

El *not* va **antes** del *to*: *told us **not to** touch*, nunca *told us to not touch* en un examen ni *told us don't touch*.

## Los verbos de reporte y sus patrones

*say* y *tell* son neutros. Los verbos de reporte dicen **qué tipo** de cosa se dijo: una sugerencia, una disculpa, una negación... Cada uno tiene **su** estructura, y eso es lo que pregunta el examen.

| Patrón | Verbos | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| verbo + ***to*** + base | *agree, refuse, offer, promise, threaten, decide, claim* | *He **refused to sign** the contract.* / *She **offered to help**.* |
| verbo + persona + ***to*** + base | *advise, ask, tell, warn, remind, encourage, persuade, invite, order, urge* | *My manager **advised me to take** the course.* / *She **reminded me to update** the ticket.* |
| verbo + ***-ing*** | *deny, admit, suggest, recommend, mention* | *He **denied deleting** the file.* / *She **suggested using** a cache.* |
| verbo + preposición + ***-ing*** | *apologise **for**, insist **on**, object **to**, confess **to*** | *He **apologised for missing** the meeting.* / *She **insisted on reviewing** every line.* |
| verbo + persona + preposición + ***-ing*** | *accuse **of**, blame **for**, congratulate **on**, thank **for**, warn **against**, criticise **for*** | *They **accused him of leaking** the data.* / *I **thanked her for helping** me.* |
| verbo + ***that*** + oración | *explain, admit, claim, complain, announce, suggest, recommend, insist* | *She **explained that** the API was deprecated.* |

::: ojo
**Las trampas más preguntadas:**

| Mal | Bien |
|---|---|
| *She suggested **me to use** a cache.* | *She **suggested using** a cache.* / *She **suggested (that) I use / should use** a cache.* |
| *He explained **me** the problem.* | *He **explained the problem to me**.* / *He **explained to me** that...* |
| *They recommended **me to** read the docs.* | *They **recommended reading** the docs.* / *They **recommended (that) I read** the docs.* |
| *He denied **to delete** the file.* | *He **denied deleting** the file.* |
| *She apologised **to be** late.* | *She **apologised for being** late.* |
| *He insisted **to pay**.* | *He **insisted on paying**.* |
:::

**Con *suggest*, *recommend* e *insist* + *that*,** el verbo puede ir en forma base para todas las personas (subjuntivo, unidad 15): *She suggested that he **review** the code* (sin *-s*), o con *should*: *that he **should review***.

## Ejemplos en inglés técnico

- *The client **said** the application **was** too slow on mobile devices.*
- *The recruiter **told me** the technical test **would take** about 40 minutes.*
- *She **asked whether** we **had considered** using a vector database.*
- *Could you tell me **where the documentation is**?*
- *The team lead **suggested migrating** the service to Kubernetes.*
- *The vendor **denied having** access to our data.*
- *The CTO **warned us not to rely** on a single cloud provider.*

## Preguntas tipo examen

::: pregunta Choose the correct option: "I'm working on the authentication module," she said. → She said that she ___ on the authentication module.
- [ ] is working
- [x] was working
- [ ] has been working
- [ ] works
::: solucion Explicación
El verbo que reporta está en pasado (*said*), así que el tiempo retrocede un paso: *present continuous* → ***past continuous***. *am working* → *was working*.
:::
:::

::: pregunta Choose the correct option: "We will release the update tomorrow," he said. → He said they ___ the update ___.
- [ ] will release / tomorrow
- [ ] would release / tomorrow
- [x] would release / the next day
- [ ] released / the next day
::: solucion Explicación
Dos cambios: ***will → would*** y ***tomorrow → the next day*** (o *the following day*), porque el "mañana" de quien lo dijo ya no es mañana para quien lo reporta.
:::
:::

::: pregunta Choose the correct option: "My manager ___ me that the deadline had been moved."
- [ ] said
- [x] told
- [ ] explained
- [ ] spoke
::: solucion Explicación
Con la **persona** directamente después del verbo (*___ me that*), el único que funciona es ***tell***: *told me that*. *said* necesitaría *said to me*, y *explained* necesitaría *explained to me*.
:::
:::

::: pregunta Choose the correct option: "Where is the staging server?" → He asked me ___.
- [ ] where is the staging server
- [x] where the staging server was
- [ ] where was the staging server
- [ ] where the staging server is it
::: solucion Explicación
En una **pregunta indirecta** el orden vuelve a ser **sujeto + verbo** (*the staging server was*), y el tiempo se corre (*is → was*). *where was the staging server* mantiene la inversión de la pregunta directa.
:::
:::

::: pregunta Choose the correct option: "Do you have experience with Docker?" → The interviewer asked me ___ experience with Docker.
- [ ] do I have
- [ ] that I had
- [x] if I had
- [ ] did I have
::: solucion Explicación
Una pregunta de **sí o no** se reporta con ***if*** o ***whether***, sin *do* y con orden de afirmación: *if I had*. *that* no sirve para preguntas.
:::
:::

::: pregunta Which sentence is correct?
- [ ] Could you tell me what time does the meeting start?
- [ ] Could you tell me what time starts the meeting?
- [x] Could you tell me what time the meeting starts?
- [ ] Could you tell me what time did the meeting start?
::: solucion Explicación
En una **pregunta indirecta de cortesía** (*Could you tell me...?*), la segunda parte va con orden de afirmación y sin *do*: *what time **the meeting starts***. El signo de pregunta del final corresponde a *Could you tell me*.
:::
:::

::: pregunta Choose the correct option: "Don't share your credentials with anyone," the admin said. → The admin told us ___ our credentials with anyone.
- [ ] don't share
- [ ] to not sharing
- [x] not to share
- [ ] not sharing
::: solucion Explicación
Órdenes negativas: ***tell*** + persona + ***not to*** + base. El *not* va **antes** de *to*.
:::
:::

::: pregunta Choose the correct option: "Why don't we use a cache?" → She suggested ___ a cache.
- [ ] us to use
- [ ] to use
- [x] using
- [ ] that we using
::: solucion Explicación
***suggest*** va con ***-ing*** (*suggested using*) o con *that* + oración (*suggested that we use / should use*). **Nunca** con persona + *to*: *suggested us to use* es uno de los errores más preguntados.
:::
:::

::: pregunta Choose the correct option: "It wasn't me! I didn't delete the branch," he said. → He ___ the branch.
- [ ] refused deleting
- [x] denied deleting
- [ ] denied to delete
- [ ] apologised to delete
::: solucion Explicación
Negar haber hecho algo es ***deny*** + ***-ing***. *refuse* es negarse a hacer algo (*refuse to do*), que no es lo mismo. *apologise* es disculparse, y va con *for* + *-ing*.
:::
:::

::: pregunta Choose the correct option: "I'm sorry I missed the stand-up," she said. → She apologised ___ the stand-up.
- [ ] to miss
- [ ] for miss
- [x] for missing
- [ ] of missing
::: solucion Explicación
***apologise for*** + ***-ing***. Después de una preposición (*for*), el verbo va siempre con *-ing*.
:::
:::

::: pregunta Choose the correct option: "You should take the advanced Python course," my mentor said. → My mentor ___ the advanced Python course.
- [ ] suggested me to take
- [x] advised me to take
- [ ] advised me taking
- [ ] recommended me to take
::: solucion Explicación
***advise*** + persona + ***to*** + base: *advised me to take*. *suggest* y *recommend* **no** usan persona + *to* (serían *suggested/recommended taking* o *that I take*).
:::
:::

::: pregunta Choose the correct option: "I've already fixed the issue," the developer said. → The developer said he ___ the issue.
- [ ] has already fixed
- [x] had already fixed
- [ ] already fixes
- [ ] would already fix
::: solucion Explicación
*Present perfect* → ***past perfect***: *have fixed → had fixed*. El verbo que reporta está en pasado (*said*).
:::
:::

::: pregunta Choose the correct option: "Can you send me the logs?" my colleague asked. → My colleague asked me ___ the logs.
- [ ] if I send him
- [x] to send him
- [ ] that I sent him
- [ ] sending him
::: solucion Explicación
Es un **pedido** (*Can you...?*), no una pregunta real: se reporta con ***ask*** + persona + ***to*** + base. *if I could send him* también sería correcto, pero *if I send him* no corre el tiempo.
:::
:::

::: pregunta Choose the correct option: "Do you know who ___ the production database?"
- [ ] did delete
- [x] deleted
- [ ] did deleted
- [ ] has deleting
::: solucion Explicación
*who* es el **sujeto** de *delete*: el orden ya es el de una afirmación y no se usa *do* (como en *Who deleted the database?*, unidad 1). En la pregunta indirecta queda igual: *who **deleted***.
:::
:::
